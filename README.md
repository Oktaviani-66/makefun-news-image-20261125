Every map needs a meridian — one line everything else is measured
against. This page takes Tuesday 25 November 2026 as its meridian and
measures the whole day against it: nothing is printed that did not
happen, or was not first reported, on that date. Thirty-seven stories
passed the measurement. They are the page.

The standard is set at the top by three stories. NATO's early-warning
jets for Poland lead, because a deployment answered by a chancellor's
promise of complete support is the day's firmest fact. The Hormuz
tanker story runs second and wears its uncertainty openly — Iran
*says* three tankers were mined, and the card is tagged CLAIMED,
because a standard that prints claims as facts is no standard at all.
Baghdad's order to seize the Kurdish oilfields closes the trio, with
President Barzani's refusal quoted in full.

Ten more cards hold the rest of the day to the same line: the 700,000
count of Russian war dead, Gaza's seven killed, Brussels' new
sanctions, Nvidia's trillion-dollar Monday (labelled Monday, because
that is when it closed), a PCE report that was due and had not
arrived — its card prints a question mark where the figure would go —
Madrid's five goals, twelve coral species returned to the living,
fifty Boeing jets, the Thanksgiving storms, and the Solomons reaching
for a different treaty.

Around them, the instruments of measurement: a strip of the day's
eight load-bearing numbers, region counts that add up to the stories
actually shown, twenty-four briefs in strict number order, six hours
of the day in sequence, a dotted map pinned by card number, and a
DATE CHECK box that holds five stories up to the meridian and finds
them wanting — a game date, last month's climate talks, a weekend
score, two-day-old science, and a sale that starts Friday.

Colophon: the page is drawn by `compose_meridian.py`, which lays the
same locked pool of the day's reporting out two ways; the black-band
layout survived the comparison and is the one in `entry.png`.
`main.py` performs the submission's only trick, which is no trick:
it copies the finished page to `/output/entry.png`, byte for byte.
Wire reporting by RFE/RL, Al Jazeera, DW, Starlit and Forbes; the
drawings are original to this page.
